# Oddmash copyright and licensing

Identify the intended use, distribution, project license and source of external code, fonts, imagery, icons, audio and datasets.

- Confirm actual license or permission; public visibility alone does not grant reuse. Record origin, version, author, license and required notices.
- Check distribution obligations and relevant dependency licenses. Preserve attribution and notices; escalate unclear or incompatible terms instead of silently relicensing.
- Distinguish linking, quoting, adapting and redistribution. Avoid copying proprietary assets or substantial code without permission; generated content is not automatically free of third-party rights.
- Verify upstream license files, document uncertainty and propose authorized alternatives. Seek qualified legal advice for material ambiguity.

## Fair use, platform rules and acceptable use

- Distinguish copyright exceptions (including jurisdiction-specific fair use/fair dealing), express licenses, platform API terms, acceptable-use rules and rate/quota policies. None automatically overrides another. For US fair use, assess purpose/character, nature, amount/substantiality and market effect together; never assume attribution, noncommercial use, a fixed percentage or AI generation establishes permission. Consult current local law and qualified counsel for uncertain or consequential uses. US reference: https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/more-info.html .
- Maintain an asset/dependency register: source, author, license version, permission evidence, attribution text, modifications, distribution scope, trademark restrictions and replacement plan. Include screenshots, gameplay/media, fonts, icon packs, user uploads, datasets and generated assets. Verify provider terms before scraping, caching, storing or redistributing data; use approved API access and respect limits.
- For services accepting user content, define applicable acceptable-use rules, content rights, reporting/removal, appeal, repeat abuse and moderation contact; do not promise moderation capacity that does not exist. Establish policies for misuse, automated traffic, quota/rate limits and fair allocation only when the product requires them, explain thresholds and recovery paths accurately.
- When terms of service are needed, draft from actual product behavior: operator, eligibility, permitted use, accounts, fees/refunds/cancellation if applicable, IP, user content, enforcement, termination, dispute/jurisdiction and policy changes. Flag decisions needing legal review; never fabricate terms or publish without authorization.
- Block distribution of assets with unresolved rights or material terms conflicts. Prefer licensed substitutes, original assets or links; document notices in shipped artifacts.
