# Feedback motion

Use [motion design](../oddmash-motion-design/SKILL.md) and [accessibility](../oddmash-accessibility/SKILL.md). Feedback represents a real state, never a simulated success.

- For clipboard or simple confirmations, show the local action state immediately when the operation resolves and a compact nonblocking toast when useful. Include a concise success/error message and non-color cue when it improves recognition; an icon is optional. Avoid duplicate screen-reader announcements when a local live region already announces the same result.
- Enter and exit a toast with subtle opacity and optionally small travel or scale over 150 to 250 ms. For bounded stacks, choose a motion that remains inside the committed layout bounds; do not introduce transient scrollbars or clip focus. Keep real overflow scrollable when viewport height requires it and sample both scroll axes during rapid entry and dismissal. Keep simple success visible about 2 to 4 seconds and preserve focus. Reuse the product's shared toast component where compatible. When users need distinct results, use a bounded stack with a product-appropriate capacity, often three or four entries, for independently completed actions; give each item its own lifetime, dismissal and stable identity. Replace or deduplicate only when the product intentionally treats updates as a single evolving operation. Pause expiry during hover or focus when an item is interactive. Important failure or actionable feedback must remain usable long enough and offer suitable dismissal or recovery.
- For stacked notifications, design the sequence as one interaction, not independent entrance effects. Use one compact visual hierarchy: recognizable status cue, concise title, optional description and dismissal with an adequate hit area. Match the product's surface, radius, spacing and typography roles; avoid extra decorative borders or glyph clutter. Choose flat or overlapping stacks according to task urgency and available space; do not prescribe stacking for a single confirmation.
- When an item enters, exits or changes height, retain stable item identity and animate the affected positions from actual geometry to the committed layout; use [layout transitions](../oddmash-motion-layout-transition/SKILL.md) for measured reflow. Keep entry, exit and repositioning coordinated, usually within 150 to 300 ms, and retarget interrupted motion from the current visual state. Avoid a jump when a middle item is removed or a new item arrives while another is moving. If an overlapping stack is chosen, keep the newest or highest-priority item readable, reveal all items on hover and keyboard focus, and provide equivalent access on touch. Never hide a focused control behind another card. Remove dismissed content and controls from accessible announcements and keyboard order immediately while retaining only a non-interactive exit presentation if needed.
- For async content, reserve realistic layout space, use meaningful progress only when it can be measured, and stop loading animation as soon as data or an error is ready. Do not add artificial delays. Avoid flashing or endless decorative skeletons.
- Cancel stale responses and timers, avoid duplicate announcements of the same event across local and global feedback, handle fast retries and changing pages, and use an immediate static presentation in reduced motion.
- Verify real success and failure, rapid repetition, expiry/dismissal, keyboard focus, narrow viewport placement, screen-reader announcement path and reduced motion. For stacks, record the rendered sequence from one to multiple items, expansion and collapse, removal of a middle and final item, rapid open/close, variable-height or localized text, and a short viewport. Check settled bounds, overflow on both axes, focus continuity and readable actionable content. A single-card screenshot, successful class assertion or entry-only animation does not establish stack quality. A single confirmation needs only applicable feedback checks, not a fabricated stack.

- Use layout-matched skeletons for cold content loads when meaningful content is not yet available; include an accessible loading label and preserve layout geometry. Do not replace already-rendered content with a skeleton or text-only loading screen during background refresh. Keep existing content and indicate refreshing unobtrusively; on failure show a useful retry and identify stale data. Verify cold loading, warm route return, refresh and failure in the rendered UI.
